Costs

What it costs to travel in Bangladesh

Bangladesh is inexpensive by most international standards — but "cheap" depends entirely on how you travel. Here are honest daily budgets for three styles, and the few things that cost more than you'd expect.

Daily budget by style (per person)

These cover a typical day of hotel, food and local movement — before big one-offs like the Sundarbans cruise or a domestic flight.

The costs that surprise people

  • The Sundarbans cruise. Easily the biggest single line for most trips, because the price bundles the boat, guide, permits and forest fees. See the full breakdown.
  • Domestic flights. Cheap by global standards, but they add up if you fly several legs instead of taking buses and trains.
  • Guides & permits in restricted areas. Parts of the Hill Tracts require both.

Where you barely spend

  • Street food and local restaurants are remarkably cheap and often the highlight.
  • Local transport — rickshaws, buses, trains — costs very little.
  • An eSIM for data is a few dollars; travel insurance is the one small cost worth never skipping.

Common questions

Is Bangladesh cheap to travel in?

Yes, by most standards. Backpackers travel on a modest daily budget; comfort travellers still find hotels and meals inexpensive by international norms. The Sundarbans cruise and domestic flights are the exceptions.

How much per day?

Budget travellers can plan for a low daily figure; comfort travellers noticeably more; premium more again. The table above gives ranges — the planner gives your currency.
