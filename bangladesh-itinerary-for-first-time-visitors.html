Bangladesh Itinerary for First-Time Visitors

First time in Bangladesh? Start where you land — Dhaka — then pair it with the Sundarbans mangrove cruise and the tea hills of Srimangal. Where to start

You'll almost certainly land in Dhaka — it's the country's main gateway and the hub everything connects from. Give it two days at the start (Old Dhaka, the Buriganga river, Sonargaon) and it stops being an obstacle and becomes part of the story.

The classic first trip

  • Dhaka (2 days). The loud, layered capital — your arrival and orientation.
  • Sundarbans (3 days). A mangrove cruise out of Khulna — the country's signature experience. See the cost from Dhaka.
  • Sylhet & Srimangal (2 days). Tea-carpet hills, rainforest and swamp forest — the green calm after the city and the forest.

That's a full, unrushed week. The 7-day itinerary lays it out day by day with costs.

How many days do you need?

Seven days is a strong first trip. Ten to fourteen lets you add the coast or the Hill Tracts at a calmer pace — see the 2-week loop. Because overland legs are long, going deep on a few regions beats rushing all five.

Before you go: the arrival checklist

  • Visa. Check your route on the visa on arrival page (US citizens: the US visa guide) and verify officially.
  • Safety. Read the current advisory in plain language on is Bangladesh safe for tourists.
  • Connectivity. A tourist eSIM ($6–18) gets you online on landing.
  • Insurance. Budget a little per week — never skip it.
  • Budget. See what it all adds up to on the trip cost page.

Common questions

Where should a first-timer go?

Dhaka plus the Sundarbans and the Srimangal tea hills — three contrasting sides of the country in one week-long loop from the capital.

How many days do I need?

Seven for a strong first trip; ten to fourteen to add the coast or Hill Tracts calmly. Go deep on a few regions rather than rushing all five.

When should I visit for the first time?

November to February — cool, dry, and the classic window for the Sundarbans and the hills.
