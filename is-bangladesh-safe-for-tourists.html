Safety

Is Bangladesh Safe for Tourists in 2026?

For most tourists, yes — with normal caution. As of mid-2026 both the US and UK place Bangladesh at their "exercise increased caution" (Level 2) tier, mainly for occasional political demonstrations, petty crime and a general terrorism risk. The main tourist regions are usually trouble-free; the sensible playbook is to avoid protests, use registered transport, and check the official advisory before you go.

What the official advisories say

On 30 July 2026 the US State Department lowered Bangladesh from Level 3 to Level 2 — Exercise Increased Caution, citing improved conditions since 2024 while still noting risks from crime, terrorism and occasional unrest. The UK FCDO sits at a comparable level and advises against all but essential travel to the Chittagong Hill Tracts. Because these change, verify the current position yourself:

  • United States: travel.state.gov — Bangladesh advisory
  • United Kingdom: gov.uk — Bangladesh travel advice

Is Bangladesh safe for solo travellers?

Plenty of solo travellers — including women — visit without incident, but Bangladesh sees few foreign tourists, so expect curiosity and attention. Dress modestly, keep valuables discreet, use ride-hailing apps rather than flagging cars, and take extra care after dark and in crowds.

Is Dhaka safe for tourists?

Dhaka is generally safe by day with normal big-city precautions. The two things to actively avoid are political demonstrations, which can escalate quickly, and unlicensed taxis. Stick to reputable hotels and app-based rides, and the capital is very manageable.

Practical safety basics

  • Avoid political rallies and large gatherings; they can turn disruptive fast.
  • Carry good travel insurance — it's the one cost never to skip.
  • Check permit and access rules for the Hill Tracts and Saint Martin's Island before you go.
  • Keep a digital copy of your passport and visa, and note your embassy's contacts.
